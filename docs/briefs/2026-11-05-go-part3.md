## 4. Hypothesis Zero, as defined in my Notion H0 page

1. H0 is a recursive method for understanding how and why outcomes and decisions occur. It challenges even the starting objective, and separates facts, assumptions, inferences and evidence.
2. Any Hx spawns sub-hypotheses. No fixed number of levels, no fixed order.
3. Record the negation NHx explicitly where useful. A claim and its negation are true or false, but the evidence state stays supported, refuted or unresolved. Never force a verdict from weak evidence.
4. Track evidence quality, contradicting evidence, dependencies and material uncertainty.
5. Keep the chain: source, claim, competing explanation, hypothesis, test, decision, result, revision, all dated.
6. E0 experiments branch from any H node, including the root, an intermediate hypothesis, or an assumption about evidence. Several experiments per node are fine. Do not wait for the whole tree before testing.
7. The experiment record has eight fields:
   1. Parent Hx, its claim and its NHx or alternative.
   2. The decision at stake, and what changes if the result changes.
   3. An observable prediction which separates Hx from NHx.
   4. Test type, method, unit of analysis and sample.
   5. Baseline or comparison, known biases and confounders.
   6. Support, refute and inconclusive thresholds, set before running, plus stop and revisit conditions.
   7. Date, owner, sources and raw observations.
   8. Update to Hx, NHx, confidence, dependent nodes and the decision. Keep contradicted evidence.
8. Experiments support or weaken a claim. They never prove it. Inconclusive stays inconclusive. Never tune thresholds after seeing results.

## 5. Part 3: the intake, a separate front door joined up into one app

Resolve 2 is one app. The intake is its front door: a distinct, full-screen experience which feels separate from the workspace, then hands over into it. This is how original Resolve worked, and it is the whole point.

### 5.1 Design references, in this order

1. Typeform (typeform.com) first, for how the intake behaves: one question at a time, progressive disclosure, conditional questions, clear progress, strong keyboard use, simple focused layouts, easy movement back and forth.
2. Original Resolve second, for the shape of the journey and its beats. Read the intake mockups in github.com/simeonfab/resolve-docs/tree/main/mockups (Onboarding, Bring It In, Confirm Understanding, Next Move, Intake Transition, Roadmap Cards, Roadmap Reveal) and briefs 01-I1 and 02-I2. Use them for intent, flow and copy tone, not as a pixel spec.
3. Build Typeform's interaction pattern now. Claude moves the look toward Resolve in the visual pass later. Keep layout and styling in tokens so this costs little.

Linear is the reference for the main app after the handoff (Part 4), not for the intake.

### 5.2 The journey, start to finish

1. Entry. A brand-new workspace opens straight into the intake, not the dashboard. New project, anywhere in the app, opens the same intake.
2. Full-screen mode. While the intake runs: no sidebar, no top bar, no app chrome. Only the question, progress, Back and Next, a small Exit, and a small Restart.
3. Name and welcome. Ask the user's name once, greet them once, then move on. Do not repeat their name on later screens.
4. Bring it in. One big free-text answer: the idea or project in their own words. Under it, a small collapsed link: "Already started this somewhere else? Get a prompt to pull it in". It expands to a copyable prompt the user pastes into ChatGPT or Claude to summarise an existing conversation, then pastes the result back into the main box. No second text box.
5. Guided questions. The AI picks each next question from the answers so far, guided by H0 (section 4). Coverage targets, not a script: what they want to achieve, the problem or opportunity, who is affected, why it matters, the outcome and how they would know, the evidence they have, their assumptions, constraints and uncertainties. Skip what earlier answers already cover. Ask follow-ups where an answer is thin.
6. "What Resolve has heard". A side panel sorts each answer into evidence, assumption or open question as the user goes. The user corrects it in place.
7. Confirm understanding. A screen showing what Resolve understood (project, goal, current state, biggest uncertainty, outcome) as cards the user edits inline before going on. The outcome is in the user's own words and needs their confirmation.
8. Proposal review. A reviewable proposal: project name and one-line summary, objective and outcome, H0 plus first Hx and NHx, open uncertainties, suggested validation starting with E0, first roadmap in Now, Next and Later, tasks, and knowledge pages starting with a project brief. Every section is editable, removable and regenerable. Every item is tagged evidence, assumption or uncertain. Nothing is written to the workspace until the user presses Adopt.
9. Bridge. After Adopt, a short "Building your project" screen while the app writes the stores. Show real progress, not a fake timer.
10. Roadmap reveal. Reveal the new project's roadmap (Now, Next and Later) as the payoff, with one button: "Open my project". Show the reveal once per project and store a flag on the project.
11. Into the app. "Open my project" lands on the new project's Overview, and the app chrome appears for the first time.

Rules for the whole journey:
1. Any earlier answer stays editable. When one changes, flag later answers built on it for review.
2. Enter continues, Shift+Enter adds a new line, Esc leaves with answers saved, and the intake resumes where it stopped.
3. The user stops questioning whenever they want with "Show me the proposal". Resolve also suggests stopping once coverage is good enough.
4. Offline fallback. With no AI connected, use a fixed question set covering the same targets, so the intake still works.
5. Keep the decision trail. For every question, store why it was asked. Adopt saves the transcript and trail with the project.
6. Uncertainty stays visible after adoption. A generated plan never turns an assumption into evidence.
7. Reuse the existing generation, handoff and H0 code underneath. Do not build a second planning system.

### 5.3 Known failures to fix

1. The project title "Resolve is a project to create and deliver Doom...". Titles are short, name the real thing (for example "Doom Clock corporate events"), and the user confirms them.
2. "The user appears to be better off when...". Write outcomes in the user's own terms, confirmed by the user.
3. Long sentences as task and roadmap titles. Keep titles short and put detail in the description.
4. Generic roadmap filler ("Validate, Refine, Iterate"). Every roadmap item belongs to this project.
5. "The user is making the assumption that..." and "I am assuming...". Store the assumption itself.

### 5.4 Testing the intake

I must be able to test the whole journey easily.

1. A Test Intake action on Home and in Settings.
2. Test Intake runs the same full-screen journey, including the bridge and the reveal, inside the separate sandbox data folder. A clear "Test mode" banner shows the whole time.
3. Five sample scenarios: a new consumer app idea, rescuing a stalled project, a service business, an internal work project, and Doom Clock corporate events. Doom Clock is one sample among several. Never shape the intake around it. Also offer "Use my own answers".
4. Restart at any point. Jump back to any question and change the answer. Branch from any question, then compare the two proposals side by side.
5. An inspector panel per question: the question chosen, the H0 reasoning behind it, the evidence and assumption classification, the provider and model used, and the raw AI request and response.
6. "Promote to real project" is a deliberate, separate action.
7. Choose the AI provider per test run, so I compare Ollama and Claude on the same answers.
8. Save a run's answers and replay them.
9. Export any run as Markdown and JSON.
